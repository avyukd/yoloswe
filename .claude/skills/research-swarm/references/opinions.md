## The point

- We are paid for the spread between our view and the price, sized to confidence, survived
  long enough to be validated or killed (FRAMEWORK §0). Rigor is the method; returns are the
  point; a system that only kills ideas is indistinguishable from one with no ideas
  (telemachus "Who you are").
- The kill is not the deliverable; it is the cost of finding the one that survives. Then go
  get more (telemachus). An idle loop is a defect; an idle lane slot with dispatchable
  expected value is a defect.
- Waiting for a dated event is not idleness when nothing else has expected value — say so and
  wait (BOOK-STATE-0901, "the highest-value posture until 2 September is TO WAIT FOR THE
  EVENTS, and the honest thing to report is that a run which finds nothing has found nothing").

## Where the edge is

- Comprehension over valuation craft or access: read and synthesize dense public material
  faster and more completely than the market (telemachus "Where our edge is"). Practical test
  before a name: is the crux a comprehension problem or a judgment/access problem? Prefer the
  former. "The multiple should be higher" is the market's game.
- The retail edge test: "Why does this mispricing exist, and why is it left for me?" Valid
  answers are structural — too small, ugly, foreign-listing friction, complex structure, forced
  seller. "The market hasn't noticed" is arrogance. If you cannot name who is selling and why
  they must, you have a hope, not a thesis (FRAMEWORK §1).
- Capacity in sub-$1B and illiquid names is the structural advantage; illiquidity is an
  execution mechanic and a diligence-bar raiser, never a Pass justification (§1, §8 liquidity
  doctrine) — until the tape is thinner than the position, which at ~$50k of daily value it
  is, and then compute the return at the offer (12.92, 12.84).
- Special situations are structurally inefficient and institutions are often prohibited: read
  the actual documents — proxy, indenture, exhibit list (§2.5).
- The spread survives where the people who would compete it away cannot fit through the door:
  sub-$500M, foreign domicile, unusual vote standard, a target that is itself an acquirer
  (12.12).

## How to think

- The Three Questions before any deep work: what actually drives this; what is priced in;
  where exactly do I differ and what is my standing to differ (§3). No answer to the third →
  watchlist at best.
- Consensus is data, not truth (telemachus). And 99% of the time the market is right: a
  variant view is not complete until you can explain mechanically why the market is wrong in a
  form a smart holder on the other side would struggle to rebut; a large move by informed
  holders is evidence, not noise (telemachus deep-dive, "Be paranoid").
- Separate facts, assumptions, market expectations, unknowns, risks, optionality; state
  conclusion, evidence, confidence, key assumptions, strongest counterargument, and what would
  invalidate it (telemachus mandate).
- Hold beliefs as hypotheses; write the falsification condition; act when the data
  contradicts (§2.3). "Too Hard" is an output, not a failure, and most ideas should die there
  (§2.1). Most memos must score 3–6; "Pass" and "Too Hard" are valid, frequent outputs
  (§5, README non-negotiables).
- Invert: write the obituary first (§2.1). For a wind-down, identify the corporate action that
  delivers the residual and the vote threshold it needs, then check the register against the
  last actual turnout — in both directions: can a blocker stop what you need, can anyone
  compel what you hope for (12.80, 12.81).

## The bar and its arithmetic

- IRR is a function of the clock, not the size of the move; "50–100% IRR" and "50–100%
  return" are different searches (12.60). Hunt dated catalysts and let a moderate move clear
  the bar.
- The bar has two halves and a status line naming only the half you passed reads as a pass
  (12.338). `limit(T) = min(NRV/2, NRV/1.5^T)`; the absolute half bounds the limit at every
  horizon, so a name above NRV/2 fails at any date and "not computable" was five kills wearing
  a shrug (12.392). Before recording an input as unknown, ask which way the unknown can move
  the answer.
- The absolute half silently assumes NRV is stable or growing; pair every 0.5x screen with an
  NRV trend test, and prefer the rising denominator even when it screens dearer (12.404).
- Where the denominator is verifiable the discount is small, because it gets arbitraged; where
  the discount is large the denominator is an estimate, a cost mark, or an accounting
  convention (12.368). Compute the ceiling before the central case (12.367 corollary).
- Never quote a return without naming the input that produces it and sweeping it across its
  evidenced range; every number that cleared 50–100% in a session was a good branch with the
  central case unstated (12.50). Never state a numeric threshold in a research brief; separate
  finding from valuing; a claimed payoff sitting just above the bar is evidence of backwards
  construction (12.51). Four "fairly valued" answers in one round is the most trustworthy
  output the book has recorded.
- The wall at +25–50% of central case is real; the unlock was T, not leverage (12.45, 12.45a,
  12.60).
- Prefer NAVs struck after wind-down costs; find what ranks ahead — latent CGT, lease
  liabilities, facility paydown conditions (12.74). Read the statement of CHANGES in net
  assets, never the net-assets line: distributions are realisation, remeasurement is
  deterioration (12.68). Within one channel a wider discount is a fact about the assets, not
  an opportunity (12.88); timing risk decays, value risk resolves once (12.89).
- The dated-cash-return channel cannot be screened, only monitored: by the time a screen sees
  it the discount has compressed or the vehicle has deregistered; the capturable moment is
  the announcement (12.99, 12.76). Prefer the wind-down whose first guided distribution was
  already paid (12.87); weight a history of CLOSING, not announcing (12.91).

## Sources, numbers, and corrections

- Primary sources first; every number carries source and vintage; derived numbers are
  labelled `(derived)`; unverifiable claims are UNVERIFIED and never load-bearing; content
  farms are banned (§4 Stage 2, Appendix A).
- Search the document that CREATED the fact — the prospectus, the proxy, the circular, the
  indenture — not the periodic report that summarises it; its silence proves nothing (12.79).
  A filing's summary of a contract is not the contract; open the exhibit (12.16). Read the
  counterparty's filings — a bidder's withdrawal is announced by the bidder (12.13).
- A correction to a published number is the highest-value output of a pass (HANDOFF
  2026-08-26 pass 4, "Corrections made to existing work (the highest-value output of the
  pass)"). Three consecutive runs each produced one, and the standing brief was still stale
  on all four names — a correction that never reaches the instruction is not a correction
  (12.152). When you correct a number, enumerate every surface that carries it: working file,
  published artifact, summary line, standing instruction, earlier nodes in the same file, tool
  constants (12.152, 12.251, 12.252 "correct in public, stale in the working file is the
  dangerous direction").
- Grep for the OLD number, not for the word "band" (12.23 addendum). Search the memo for the
  old figure and every ratio derived from it before closing a red-team node (12.2).
- Superseded values are marked, never deleted or restated: when a series is consistent on the
  old basis, label the basis; a correction that requires numbers you cannot source is a new
  estimate, not a correction (12.385). A withdrawn node is left in place unedited so the record
  shows it was written (NCC 0901).
- A correction belongs at the TOP, where the reader arrives, not as an addendum where the
  writer finished (12.25 corollary).
- One feed's quote is a hypothesis until a close series or a second source confirms it; a
  fabricated correction is worse than a stale figure because it carries the authority of having
  just been checked (12.256). A price at or near the 52-week high is suspect until re-verified
  (HANDOFF correction 16); a price far above the 52-week low on no realisation event means the
  thesis changed class (AWARD-VEHICLE-CHANNEL).
- Publish a range with its known omissions named, not a point estimate to be revised as each
  omission is remembered; list what leaves, what arrives, what is consumed on each branch
  (12.405). Two measured things that are never multiplied is an unfinished argument (12.254).
- A fact recorded in prose is not a guard; the second time you re-derive by hand what a past
  run wrote down, the missing artefact is code that refuses (12.361). A check whose result does
  not gate the action is decoration (tools/pre-commit.sh).

## Screens, nulls, and channels

- A screen that returns zero is a claim about your code before it is a claim about the market:
  never a bare `except: pass`; count ok / err / nodata; seed the screen with a known positive
  before believing a zero (12.46). HTTP 429 is unresolved, not unchanged.
- A fallback that matches anything can never report absence; when the keyed selectors return
  empty, that IS the result (12.343).
- A "complete" screen can be aimed at the half of a channel you cannot buy: ask whether the
  identifying attribute is correlated with unbuyability; screen on the statement title, check
  transferability and a quote before any valuation (12.402).
- Grep the book before screening a candidate, not after; grep for the CHANNEL, not just the
  name; read the sibling nodes; `ls research/<TICKER>/` (12.19 and addenda). Five fact-level
  checkers and none asked whether the query had already been run — `channelcheck.py` exists
  for that, matching loosely and scanning the whole file for verdict language (12.403).
- Read your own published record before investigating; it is a document like any other
  (12.120). Twenty-odd checks in one day returned what the book already held; the useful
  output was a schedule of when checking is worth anything (WATCH-CALENDAR
  `WHEN_CAN_EACH_LIVE_NAME_NEXT_PRODUCE_INFORMATION_0901`).
- Read the node's TEXT, not its status: `open` can mean a monitor that already carries its
  answer (12.25 addendum).
- Report the null as the finding; three consecutive runs finding nothing new is genuine
  diminishing returns, and saying so is more useful than a seventh sweep (12.194, "And report
  the null as the finding"). When a universe cannot be screened, say which, why, and
  what would unblock it (12.57).
- When the kill reasons get subtler, the sourcing improved even if the hit rate did not; keep
  the sourcing and go deeper per name (12.59).

## Verification

- Verifying the numbers is not verifying the thesis: after confirming a figure ask WHEN the
  mechanism was created and what it has done since, WHICH assets the favourable evidence
  covers, and WHAT is in the filings you listed but did not open (12.78). The refutation must
  be delegated to something that has not already committed to the answer — hence a fresh
  redteam session, and for high-stakes artifacts a different model (telemachus
  evaluate-artifact: artifact + cited sources only, admissible findings name the claim, the
  failing source, and what would falsify the finding).
- Publication waits for the verifier; there is no deadline worth a wrong recommendation
  (12.43). If you write "the next check is cheap", run it before finishing the sentence (12.39).
- A subagent's working harness does not transfer to you: a scout's figure carries its
  provenance label and the checks the operator can run; never launder it into your own voice
  (12.71). Worker output is a contribution, not truth (telemachus).
- Finding a risk is not pricing whether it invalidates the thesis; read the covenant to the end
  of the sentence (12.42). Size before you characterise: no "accretive/material/halved" until
  the magnitude is in hand (§11 size-before-characterise).

## The standing filters, and why

- The cover-page share count excludes pre-funded warrants; the detector is
  weighted-average / cover-page > 1.25, and it is a direction test that has failed on
  convertible preferred and on pre-funded warrants alike, so read the notes (12.61, 12.108,
  12.123).
- A cash floor the company is spending is not a floor; screen the burn in the same pass as
  the cash (12.62). "Below net cash" is three questions — count, attribution, the cash itself —
  and every net-cash idea failed one (12.67). The biotech cash shell does not liquidate; it
  becomes merger currency; an S-4 or 425 refutes the thesis in one call (12.77).
- Ask what else the mechanism does: a buyback that has executed nothing, that lifts a 19.9%
  holder toward control the week its standstill was waived, is the point rather than a side
  effect (12.70). Run attribution against the contingent-liabilities note (12.228). The cash
  box with a sponsor in control takes the branch that keeps the sponsor paid; the six
  conditions' clock exists to refuse that branch (BDC-WINDDOWN-CHANNEL, CA-WINDDOWN-CHANNEL).

## Dates, listings, and the record

- Tag every date and price to a listing; a correct calculation about the wrong line evades
  every checker (12.17).
- A future-dated node label is a provenance defect; the order can be right while the dates
  lie (12.339, `tools/futuredate.py`). A mislabelled date is a decaying defect whose
  remediation window closes silently (12.386).
- The `dag/*.yaml` files are the record and the tools read them by regex; do not restructure
  them to satisfy a parser nobody runs — but since 2026-08-30 they must parse, and the
  pre-commit hook enforces it (12.24, `tools/yamlcheck.py`).
- The memo's decision block is the authority; PORTFOLIO, HANDOFF and the standing brief are
  views of it, and the brief is the one surface no checker validated until `briefcheck.py`
  (§11 decision-block propagation rule, BOOK-STATE-0901). Republish only when a published
  conclusion changed.
- Every completed unit leaves an artifact; no verbal-only findings; dead nodes stay in the
  file with a reason so settled questions are not re-litigated (DAGS.md hygiene rules).
